# Two RTX 3090 s: replicas versus AR/acoustic splitting

## Setup

2026-10-04, two RTX 3090 s/595.71.05, PXB, no NVLink or bidirectional P2P access. Replicas: two native instances, AR4/KV4 GiB each. Split: AR GPU 0 with four slots, acoustic GPU 1 with one execution slot/receiver capacity 4. Explicit GPU→CPU shared-memory→GPU transfer, ACK only after destination copy completion. MPS off; same 512/768/1024/1280 frame requests, shape warmup and three rounds per concurrent load. Replica routing balances total frames.

## Results

| Load | Replica audio/s | Split audio/s | E2EL P95 replicas→split(s) | RTF P95 replicas→split |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Concurrent 4 | 8.705 | 7.420 | 16.465→19.347 | 0.353→0.378 |
| Concurrent 8 | 12.059 | 8.128 | 23.737→35.236 | 0.464→0.821 |
| Continuous 16/cap 8 | 12.195 | 8.517 | 27.129→48.876 | 0.695→1.308 |

Continuous throughput fell 30.16%, E2EL P95 rose about 80.16%. Split-device sampled utilization was 45.36% AR/93.80% acoustic, versus 90.02%/94.76% for replicas. Transfers averaged 1.945790 s per 16 requests (about 0.122 s each), excluding pre-Prepare waits; those durations overlap acoustic work and cannot simply be subtracted to predict P2P gains.

All 182 outputs passed length/finite/error checks. Four transport fixtures passed exactness, source-reuse-after-ACK, capacity/discard/reuse checks. A single 1024 frame split trial was 10.979461 s versus 11.182929 s with one replica active; no stable latency claim.

## Scope

The split uses CPU staging, with four AR slots versus eight across the replicas. The tested 1: 1 split showed no throughput advantage. Role allocation, resource-ratio tuning, natural-EOS quality and in-flight cancellation/shutdown remained open.

## Reproduction and evidence

Original `scripts/cross_gpu_transport.py`, then `scripts/run.py --arm split` / `--arm replicas` with new labels and the explicit transport patch.
