# Acoustic stream priority: small descriptive change, waits remain

## Setup

2026-10-09, H800, MPS off. Only acoustic stream priority changes from highest −3 to 0; MAX_INFLIGHT2 unchanged. Equal→highest, 64 matched prompts/seeds plus two warmups each; AR32/KV32 GiB/client 64/NAR1/VAE1/window 0/fullCoT/CFG1/ABC 4096/semantic 9000. Both runs use external probes and Nsight graph-level tracing.

## Results

| Metric | Highest priority | Equal priority 0 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Audio/s | 21.7689 | 22.0137 |
| E2EL P50/P95 (s) | 367.231/610.138 | 348.828/600.848 |
| RTF P50/P95 | 1.4351/2.4643 | 1.4741/2.4570 |
| Device peak (GiB) | 66.204 | 68.089 |
| Acoustic gaps(s) | 108.934 | 98.519 |
| Largest gap(s) | 26.026 | 45.723 |
| Ready-but-not-started(ms) | 1.882 | 1.787 |

Throughput +1.12%, E2EL P95−1.52%, RTF P95−0.30%; output duration −0.724%. For the same 32-row composition (6 ABC+26 semantic), active acoustic step intervals were 54.697→54.935 ms. Major AR waits remained.

The equal-priority 45.723 s gap separates jobs for requests 0–31 from 32–63. AR stayed full, no acoustic request was ready, and later ABC work had already begun: no global wait-for-all-audio barrier. Idle/active median SM1755/1215 MHz and approximately 287/346 W were alike in both runs; no thermal slowdown.

## Scope

One profiled pair with differing contexts, acoustic shapes and instantaneous clocks. Stream priority influences scheduling without preempting active kernels. Lowering priority was not adopted; the cohort timing leaves the remaining causes of the gap unresolved.
