# Complete acoustic generation in the AR worker

## Setup

RTX 3090, driver 595.71.05, MPS off; 2026-10-04. AR4/KV4 GiB; fixed 512/768/1024/1280-frame requests. Compare native HOLD-driven acoustic progress with directly draining the existing song iterator in the same worker. Only `_hold_allowed` and `_finish_request` change; 129 other functions/methods have identical ASTs. Four shape warmups; three rounds per concurrent load; main runs before the direct variant.

## Results

| Load | main audio/s | Direct audio/s | Change | main E2EL P95 (s) | Direct E2EL P95 (s) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Concurrent 4 | 6.185 | 5.780 | -6.55% | 23.185 | 24.799 |
| Concurrent 8 | 6.365 | 5.965 | -6.29% | 45.032 | 48.033 |
| Continuous 16, cap 8 | 6.695 | 6.171 | -7.84% | 48.078 | 56.563 |

Continuous-load RTF P95 increased 1.563→1.709. A single 1024-frame trial improved 11.220622→10.744295 s (about 4.25%), without establishing repeatability. All 186 outputs passed length/finite/error checks. Four sequential FP32 waveforms were exact; only 4/84 concurrent waveform hashes matched. Device peaks were 19278/19284 MiB.

The direct loop removes HOLD advancement but blocks other requests' AR until synthesis finishes. YuE already had same-process weights and conditioning-KV reuse; this experiment did not import the full Cosmos DiffusionEngine.

## Scope

Requests used fixed frame budgets and sequential run order; semantic tokens could differ. The concurrent regression led to retaining asynchronous acoustic progress. Cancellation, shutdown and audio quality were outside this experiment.

Original `scripts/run.py --arm baseline` and `--arm direct`, with new labels; `scripts/summarize.py`.
